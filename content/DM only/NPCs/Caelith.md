**Caelith** — leader of the settlement

An elf woman who is old by the settlement's standards, perhaps a hundred and twenty, which means she was born here and has never been anywhere else. She leads through accumulated knowledge rather than authority — she simply knows more than anyone, has read every record the order brought in and every record kept since, and remembces things three generations back through oral tradition maintained with the precision her people treat as sacred obligation.

She is not warm. She is exacting, deliberate, and possessed of a particular kind of dignity that comes from people who have decided what they believe and arranged their entire existence around it. She will be respectful to the party because her faith demands hospitality. She will also be watching them very carefully.

_What she knows:_ Everything about the Lady — her nature, her history, her relationship to the life cycle. She does not know the Lady's twist. When the party discovers or reveals it, her reaction is the scene. This is a woman whose entire life, and her parents' lives, and their parents' lives, were built on a story that just changed underneath her. How she handles that is a character moment you give her, not one you script.

_What she wants:_ Confirmation. She has spent her life maintaining faith in something she cannot verify, tending a goddess whose body is dying around her. She wants the party to tell her it matters. She wants to know someone from outside came here and it wasn't too late.

_The hope:_ She believes completely. Not blindly — she has had a century of evidence that her situation is terrible, and she has kept believing anyway. That kind of faith is genuinely moving, whatever you think of its object.

_The pity:_ She was born here. She will die here. She knows both of these things and has made her peace with them in a way that is either inspiring or heartbreaking depending on when you look at it.

_The dread, very quiet:_ She knows the party is here to complete a ritual that may kill the Lady entirely. She doesn't know this consciously. But she is perceptive enough that she suspects something, and if the party stays long enough she will ask them, directly, what the ritual actually does. What they tell her is up to them.
