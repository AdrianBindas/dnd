**The Chorus** — encountered late Act III or Act IV, before or just after the ambush

Not a single being. The Chorus is what happens when the cancer fully consumes a group and they don't disappear — they persist, networked, thinking together. This particular Chorus was once six people. You can still hear them individually if you listen carefully, distinct voices cycling through a single body that is now more Belly than person — slow, large, rooted to the wall of a particular chamber like something between a person and a growth.

They are not hostile. They are not suffering, or they say they are not suffering, which is different. They speak in overlapping fragments that require patience to parse. They know the Belly intimately from the inside — literally, because they have partially become it.

_What they offer:_ Information about the deeper chambers that no one else has. They have felt the servant moving through the walls the way you feel footsteps through floorboards. They know where it's going.

_The cost:_ Spending time with the Chorus is disorienting. The party might leave the chamber uncertain which thoughts were theirs. Nothing mechanical — just the lingering feeling of having been briefly, gently, almost imperceptibly considered for membership in something vast.

_The pity:_ There are still six names in there. They remember them. They say them sometimes, each voice claiming its own, and it sounds like something that used to be pride.

_The hope, perversely:_ They are not unhappy. They say the pain stopped. They are not trying to convert the party. They are just telling the truth as they experience it, which is exactly what makes them the servant's best argument.
